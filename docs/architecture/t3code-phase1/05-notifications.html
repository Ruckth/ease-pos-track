Ticket notifications in the app, by email and through LINE

Start with a private in-app inbox. Offer optional verified email and LINE after each channel passes its delivery, consent and privacy checks. An external alert takes the recipient to the authenticated inbox.

Who receives each update

The Owner designates intake staff and backup through Project intake-membership rows keyed by Project and active staff ID. Recheck those rows before inbox display or dispatch. In the pilot, new unassigned Ticket alerts are on by default for designated intake staff; each person can opt out. A manual assignment creates a staff-only item for the new assignee. A customer-visible status change goes to the active customer who owns the Ticket and remains a member of its active Project. Save preferences by person, Project, channel and category. External channels default off and require individual verification and opt-in.

An archive event produces no customer inbox item, count, link or external intent. Immediately hide prior customer inbox items for the archived Ticket or Project and cancel unsent external work. Staff may inspect the archive through existing staff authorization. Restore can produce a new status item only after active Ticket, Project, membership, ownership and preferences all pass. Re-test removed membership, a same-Project peer and a previously authorized open inbox. In-app users can open a Ticket only after current authorization.

Inbox history after access changes

Bind external delivery to the exact consent interval. At event commit the intent stores recipient, Project, channel, category and the effective consent generation vector: the person’s channel default, Project/category override and current verified binding. Opt-out, withdrawal, replacement, deletion and re-opt-in advance the relevant monotonic generation; never recreate a prior generation when a preference returns to the same value. Freeze these immutable generations on the original logical intent and bind the dispatch permit to them. Require the same effective vector and current affirmative choice on claim, immediately before every provider call, on retry and on repair. A changed vector terminally suppresses old queued work. Re-opt-in applies only to new eligible events; historic event replay cannot mint a new intent under the new consent.

Keep provider-accepted and unknown observations for restricted reconciliation under their original identity. No retry, provider status action that initiates a send, or manual requeue may transmit under withdrawn consent. Non-sending reconciliation can use incoming verified evidence. The bounded attempt whose final authorization committed before withdrawal may still start; record its outcome and never reissue it under withdrawn consent. Test withdrawal and re-opt-in on the same verified destination before resuming a paused worker. Old queued and unknown items must yield zero outbound send calls, unchanged budget and no new intent; a later eligible event may send once. Test changes at both the shared default and Project override.

Archive and loss of Project membership permanently suppress existing customer inbox items and counts for the affected access interval. They never reappear after restore or rejoining. Bind each item and pending external intent to the Ticket, Project and membership visibility generations at the event's commit. Advance the relevant generation in the same transaction as archive, removal, restore or new membership. Render and count only current generations with live access. Old generations are terminal even before a bounded cleanup pass marks rows suppressed. The worker cancels unstarted sends with stale generations, never retries their provider calls and preserves accepted or unknown history for authorized reconciliation.

Restore may create one new restore item only for a customer with current access and preferences. Rejoining alone generates no historical items or outbound alerts. Rehearse Ticket and Project archive/restore and membership removal/re-addition with an open subscription, a prior unread row, a pending send and a provider request already in flight. Check list, unread count, direct lookup, external retry and re-enable behavior. Previously accepted messages remain part of the limited provider history.

Optional email and LINE

The staff identity sender has a separate Resend credential, budget and enable switch. Ticket email uses a separately verified sender, one recipient per request and a short-lived code entered in authenticated app settings. Recheck recent action proof on contact changes, verify the new destination, and bump its binding generation on replace or unlink. Neither Google email nor a customer's username enrolls the person in Ticket notifications. Resend domains

For LINE use the business-owned Official Account's Messaging API. Link a single one-to-one user using the provider-issued link token and an app-generated short-lived nonce bound to the authenticated person and binding generation. Verify the signed accountLink event, destination, result and nonce; consume once. A friend or follow event alone does not link an identity. Separate linking from category opt-in. Staff sign in with Google in the system browser using an ordinary HTTPS handoff with openExternalBrowser=1 or explicit Open in browser fallback. One delivery identity across replay and restore

Host the optional alert dispatcher in the existing server deployment as a separately permissioned server-only process next to the Hono upload service. Use its own service identity and secret configuration; it shares neither upload callback authority nor browser credentials. During restore default to stopping the sender and revoking/rotating its provider credentials before recovery. The independent epoch prevents a new worker from transmitting an old intent even after new credentials are installed. The manual pilot needs no external Ticket delivery process.

External Ticket delivery requires a separately controlled outbound dispatcher. Only this process holds Resend/LINE Ticket-sending credentials and network egress. An independent durable control store outside the Convex backup holds a monotonic dispatch epoch and open/closed state. Default closed if the store or epoch cannot be verified. Restore operators close the interlock and advance the epoch before replacing data. Stop old workers and drain or terminate any already-started sends; mark ambiguous outcomes. The dispatcher serializes close with starting a provider request and verifies the independent epoch at the actual socket handoff. Workers cannot contact providers directly or use a locally cached permit to bypass it.

Every committed database attempt also carries the independent epoch and a unique executor identity. On restart compare both database and dispatcher epochs under current approved configuration. New attempts cannot reuse old executor grants. A restored “enabled” flag cannot open the interlock. Recover the manifest and reconcile observed outcomes while closed. In both evidence branches install the fresh activation and consent boundary below. Old work remains permanently ineligible when an operator opens the new epoch. If the external control store is itself lost or the old workers cannot be fenced, leave delivery off and remove or rotate the sending credentials before recovery.

Restore a snapshot showing delivery enabled, an old consent vector and queued work while a surviving worker retains a pre-restore successful authorization response. After external close and epoch rotation, let the worker resume, re-opt in and replay that old event. The recording transport must show zero new sends, new intents and charges; a genuinely new eligible event after deliberate reactivation sends once. Also test the close racing a socket already started: record at most that one pre-close side effect and reconcile it without reissue.

Use exactly one stable logical intent key: (immutable event ID, immutable recipient ID, channel). Each event has exactly one category per recipient/channel in Phase 1. Consent vectors, category, binding generation, visibility generations, frozen destination/payload digest and provider idempotency identity are immutable facts on that intent. They never make a second intent for the same key. Create the record, or a minimal terminal suppression for an evaluated ineligible recipient, only while committing the original event. Duplicate event processing resolves the original decision without re-evaluating under today’s consent or charging budget again. New opt-in affects new events only.

Keep the immutable event sequence and durable processed-through watermark per Project/channel. A bounded gap ledger covers committed decisions above the contiguous watermark. Advance it only across fully sealed event decisions. Keep necessary non-secret per-intent tombstones until covered by that watermark and beyond provider retry/reconciliation windows. A replay at or below the watermark is a no-op even after the 30-day diagnostics expire. The event commit seals the original recipient set; reprocessing cannot add a new recipient. Deduplicate budget reservations and settlement by attempt ID and logical intent. Count an accepted or unknown send conservatively once against its original budget.

On every restore, including a successful reconciliation, burn the previous channel activation interval. While the independent dispatcher remains closed, advance its external epoch and install a fresh activation boundary and consent epoch in the database. This boundary is mandatory even if every pre-restore receipt is accounted for. Old intents never transfer to the new interval. Any pre-boundary event, intent, restored pending row, spent permit or dead-letter entry is permanently send-ineligible. This includes a fresh worker claim, retry, manual repair, import, backfill and replay. An old accepted or unknown outcome may receive verified status evidence. Such reconciliation makes zero send requests and creates no replacement intent or new charge.

At event commit bind the immutable event and intent to the currently open activation interval. Current activation and independent dispatch epochs must match in intent creation, queue claim, retry, repair, begin-attempt and the outbound dispatcher. Compare event lineage as well as sequence, so a counter rewind cannot admit an older event. Do not update an old row to a new epoch. Clear external opt-ins from operational use after restore; require fresh affirmative channel consent and current destination proof in the new interval. Reopening enables only genuinely new eligible events committed after the activation boundary and renewed consent. If a fresh namespace, epochs or consumer interlock cannot be proven, leave dispatch closed. In-app access recovery is separate from outbound transmission.

Exercise both complete and incomplete evidence paths. Snapshot an enabled channel with verified consent and a queued intent, withdraw consent after that snapshot, close the independent interlock, rotate epoch, restore and deliberately reopen with a new activation boundary. Start a brand-new worker with no cached permit. Try claim, retry, direct begin, repair and old-event replay, both before and after fresh opt-in on the same destination. Assert zero old sends, replacement intents and new charges. Deliver a newly committed post-boundary event exactly once at the instrumented fake-provider transport. Replay it and assert the same logical intent. Repeat with an old unknown row and an old surviving worker. Reopening with incomplete boundary evidence must fail closed.

Use the shared external-action ordering test for each channel. A worker paused before its final database authorization may send nothing after withdrawal. If its one-time authorization already committed first, the live worker may start that one frozen request within five seconds, even if the person opts out before the provider call. A subsequent retry requires a new authorization; opt-out suppresses it permanently for that intent. Non-sending reconciliation remains available.

Delivery, privacy and retention

Create an audience-scoped event and idempotent inbox intent with the committed Ticket mutation. Enforce current access at render. Only when a channel is approved add an external outbox. Use the stable event/recipient/channel key and store the original binding generation and provider request identity as immutable fields. Use a leased worker with a monotonic fence and per-attempt checks for active audience, binding, preferences, freshness, budget and channel switch. This preserves authorization through retries. App writes and notifications have independent kill switches. The minimum-pilot schema omits external outbox, quiet hours, grouping, digests and reply handling.

An external alert uses a fixed template and a generic authenticated /inbox URL on the approved origin. Include no Ticket-specific locator in the subject, body, URL, preheader, preview metadata or tracking redirect. Disable tracking pixels and open/click redirects. After sign-in the inbox resolves its current items. A link preview has no side effects. Signatures and current membership do not retract a message accepted immediately before a preference or access change. Tell the recipient this when enabling the channel.

Retry transient failures with the same provider identity and frozen payload. Distinguish queued, accepted, delivered if evidenced, suppressed, permanently failed and unknown. Stop after five attempts or six hours of freshness, cap routine sends at 20 per recipient/channel/day, and respect provider budgets. Resend and LINE push retry identities are valid for 24 hours. Never resend an ambiguous expired request with a fresh key. Verify raw webhook signatures, destination and provider event ID, then process durably and idempotently; reconcile delayed or out-of-order events. Test unknown acceptance and no duplicate provider-side effects with fakes. Resend idempotency · Resend webhook verification · LINE retry semantics · LINE webhook signatures

Delete inbox items and sanitized delivery diagnostics after 30 days and minimal security audit after 90 days, subject to the controller's recorded approval and documented holds. Minimize dedupe/suppression evidence and keep no reusable unlinked identifier. Test controlled-clock expiry, backups, isolated unknown outcomes, generic URL content, suppression after archive and no historical replay on restored data. The privacy lead completes the controller, transfer, rights and incident gates before real enrollment and separately before enabling each external channel.
